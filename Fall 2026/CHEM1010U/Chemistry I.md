## Fall 2026

## 1. General course summary

CHEM1010 introduces foundational chemistry, including:

- Chemical tools, experimentation, and measurement
- The periodic table and naming
- Atoms, molecules, and ions
- The mole and unit conversion
- Mass relationships and stoichiometry
- Reactions in aqueous solution
- Acids, bases, salts, and titration
- Gases
- Electronic structure and periodicity
- Ionic and covalent bonding
- Lewis structures and VSEPR theory
- Molecular structure and bonding theories
- Organic and biological chemistry

The course combines lectures, online quizzes, tutorials, laboratory sessions, practice problems, CHEMPass, a midterm, and a final exam.

## 4. Week-by-week requirements and preparation

> The syllabus provides a provisional topic sequence rather than a fully dated weekly calendar. The following plan maps the listed lecture sequence onto the semester. Confirm the exact Canvas module and laboratory activity for each week.

### Week 1 — Introduction, measurement, and atoms
**Study**
- Course expectations and laboratory procedures.
- Measurement, units, significant figures, precision, and accuracy.
- Scientific notation and dimensional analysis.
- Atoms, molecules, and ions.
- Naming basic chemical substances.

**Do**
- Obtain the textbook and laboratory manual.
- Review Canvas modules before class.
- Practise unit conversions and significant-figure rules.
- Complete the first weekly quiz.

**Expect**
- Foundational calculation questions.
- Laboratory safety and measurement expectations.

### Week 2 — The mole and chemical formulas
**Study**
- The mole and Avogadro’s number.
- Molar mass.
- Converting between mass, moles, and particles.
- Chemical formulas and naming.

**Do**
- Practise multi-step mole conversions.
- Calculate molar masses.
- Complete the scheduled laboratory preparation.

**Expect**
- Numerical quiz questions requiring clear units.

### Week 3 — Mass relationships and stoichiometry
**Study**
- Balancing chemical equations.
- Mole ratios.
- Stoichiometric calculations.
- Limiting reactants.
- Theoretical yield and percent yield.

**Do**
- Solve reaction-mass problems.
- Identify the limiting reactant.
- Practise explaining each calculation step.
- Complete tutorial problems.

**Expect**
- Multi-step calculations and common unit-conversion traps.

### Week 4 — Reactions in aqueous solution
**Study**
- Aqueous solutions and electrolytes.
- Solubility and precipitation.
- Molecular, complete ionic, and net ionic equations.
- Acid–base and redox reaction recognition where assigned.

**Do**
- Memorize or construct a solubility-rule reference.
- Practise writing net ionic equations.
- Prepare for the first major topic review.

**Expect**
- Classification of reactions and equation-writing questions.

### Week 5 — Gases
**Study**
- Pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of gas.
- Ideal gas law.
- Gas stoichiometry.
- Partial pressures and gas mixtures.
- Ideal versus non-ideal behavior.

**Do**
- Practise rearranging the ideal gas law.
- Track units carefully.
- Complete tutorial and CHEMPass work.

**Expect**
- Calculation-heavy questions.

### Week 6 — Midterm review
**Study**
- Review Chapters 1–4 and 10 topics listed before the midterm.
- Revisit measurement, moles, stoichiometry, aqueous reactions, and gases.

**Do**
- Complete mixed practice problems without notes.
- Build a formula and method sheet for personal study.
- Review laboratory concepts and tutorial errors.

**Expect**
- In-person, closed-book, paper midterm on October 26 or 27, depending on CRN.

### Week 7 — Electronic structure and periodicity
**Study**
- Atomic theory and electronic structure.
- Orbitals and electron configurations.
- Periodic trends.
- Ionization energy, electron affinity, and atomic size.

**Do**
- Practise electron configurations and ions.
- Explain trends rather than only memorizing arrows.
- Complete the assigned weekly quiz.

**Expect**
- Conceptual comparisons across elements.

### Week 8 — Ionic compounds and bonding
**Study**
- Formation of ions.
- Ionic bonding.
- Lattice energy.
- Periodic trends related to ionic compounds.

**Do**
- Compare ionic sizes and charges.
- Explain factors affecting lattice energy.
- Practise naming ionic compounds.

**Expect**
- Questions connecting periodic properties to bonding.

### Week 9 — Covalent bonds and Lewis structures
**Study**
- Covalent bonding.
- Lewis structures.
- Formal charge.
- Resonance.
- Octet-rule exceptions where assigned.

**Do**
- Draw Lewis structures step by step.
- Check formal charges.
- Practise structures with multiple bonds and resonance.

**Expect**
- Structure-drawing and reasoning questions.

### Week 10 — Molecular structure and VSEPR
**Study**
- VSEPR theory.
- Electron-domain geometry.
- Molecular geometry.
- Bond angles.
- Polarity.

**Do**
- Build a geometry table.
- Predict shape and polarity from Lewis structures.
- Complete tutorial problems.

**Expect**
- Diagram-based questions.

### Week 11 — Bonding theories
**Study**
- Bonding theories covered in the course.
- Valence-bond concepts.
- Molecular orbital theory as assigned.
- Bond order and molecular properties.

**Do**
- Compare bonding models.
- Practise interpreting molecular structures.
- Review difficult concepts with the instructor or TA.

**Expect**
- Questions asking why a model predicts a particular property.

### Week 12 — Organic and biological chemistry
**Study**
- Functional groups.
- Naming and identifying organic molecules.
- Properties of biologically important molecules.
- Connections between chemistry and biological systems.

**Do**
- Make a functional-group reference sheet.
- Identify structures from names and names from structures.
- Complete the relevant CHEMPass and quiz work.

**Expect**
- Recognition and classification questions.

### Week 13 — Review and final preparation
**Study**
- Integrate stoichiometry, reactions, gases, atomic structure, periodicity, and bonding.
- Revisit all laboratory and tutorial concepts.

**Do**
- Complete remaining quizzes, tutorials, CHEMPass, and lab requirements.
- Practise full-length mixed problems.
- Check the official examination schedule.

**Expect**
- Final review and preparation for a final exam similar in format to the midterm.

- Exact date, time, and room are published in the official examination schedule.
- Bring the required current photo Student ID.
