# Chapter 1: Chemical Tools: Experimentation & Measurement
* **Chemistry**: the study of composition, properties, and transformations of matter.
* Chemistry is deeply involved in both the changes that take place in nature and the profound social changes of the past two centuries. No educated person today can understand the modern world without a basic knowledge of chemistry.  
### 1.1 The Scientific Method
* An iterative process involving the formulation of questions arising from observations, careful design of experiments, and thoughtful analysis of results.
  * The main elements include:
    1.  **Observations**: which are systematic recording of natural phenomena and may be qualitative, descriptive in nature, or quantitative involving measurements.
    2.  **Hypothesis** which is a possible explanation for the observation developed based upon facts collected from previous experiments as well as scientific knowledge and intuition. The hypotheisis may not be correct, but it must be testable with an experiment.
    3.  **Experiment** which is a procedure for testing the hypothesis. Experiments are most useful when they are performed in a controlled manner, meaning that only one variable is changed at a time while all others remain constant.
    4.  **Theory** which is developed from a hypothesis consistent with experimental data and is a unifying principle that explains experimental results. It also makes predictions about related systems, and new experiments are carried out to verify the thoery.
  * It's important to note that theories only ever act as the best possible explanation that can be made at the present time. Never absolutely true.
### 1.2 Measurements: SI Units and Scientific Notation
* SI Units, based on the metric system, are the standard units of measurement in all industrialized countries (barring the USA).
* The seven fundamnetal include:
  * **Mass**: Kilogram, kg
  * **Length**: meter, m
  * **Temperature**: kelvin, K
  * **Amount of Substance**: mole, mol
  * **Time**: second, s
  * **Electric Current**: ampere, A
  * **Luminous Intensity**: candela, cd
* due to the huge range these qualities can have; prefixes are often used to simplify the recording of the measurements. Giga, Kilo, milli, nano to name a few.
* Scientific notation is standard in recording numbers as well:
  * ie. 55,000,000 becomes 55 * 10^6 and 0.00320 becomes 3.20 * 10^(-3).
  * numbers always are accompanied by their unit, to give scope.
### 1.3 Mass and Its Measurement
* **Mass** is defined as the amount of matter in an object.
  * **Matter** is a catchall term used to describe anything with physical presence you can touch, taste, smell or simply put, anything with mass.
* Mass is measured in kg, ecause the kilogram is too large for many purposes in chemistry, the metric gram (g), the milligram (mg) , and the microgram (μg)  are more commonly used.
* Mass and weight are often used interchangeably, though have quite different meanings.
  * Mass is a physical property measuring the amount of matter in an object.
  * Weight measures the force with which gravity pulls on an object.
### 1.4 Length and Its Measurement
* the meter (m) is the standard for length.
* about 39.37 inches, 10% longer than an english yard and too large for most measurements in chemistry.
* Because the kilogram is too large for many purposes in chemistry, the metric gram (g) , the milligram (mg), and the microgram (μg), and the picometer (pm)  are more commonly used.
### 1.5 Temperature and Its Measurement
* the kelvin (K) is the standard for temperature.
* For all practical purposes the celsius and kelvin are both one-hundredth the interval between the freezing point of water and the boiling point of water at standard atmospheric pressure.
* Kelvin assigns 0 K to the coldest possible temperature of -273.15 C (absolute zero).
* Thus 273.15 K = 0 C.
* To get from Fharenheit to Celcius C = (5/9) * (F - 32) and from there kelving is +273.15.
### 1.6 Derived Units: Volume and Its Measurement
* Area is length * length = m^2 thus volume is area * length = m^3.
* m^3 is often too large for chemistry, more common is cm^3 = 10^(-6)m^3
### 1.7 Derived Units: Density and Its Measurement
* The relationship between the **mass** of an object and its volume is called **density**.
* **density** is calculated as the mass of an object divided by its volume and is exressed as the SI derived unity g/mL for a liquid and g/cm^3 for a solid.
* Density = mass over volume.
* Substances expand and contracted when heated or frozen thus *When reporting density, temperature must also be specified*
* Knowing the density of a liquid can be useful because it's often easier to measure a liquid by volume than by mass.
### 1.8 Derived Units: Energy and Its Measurement
* **Energy** is defined as the capacity to supply heat or do work.
* **Kinetic Energy (Ek)** is the energy of motion, the amount of kinetic energy in a moving object with mass m and velocity v is given by the equation:
  * Ek = (1/2)mv^2
  * ie. A ball falling off a cliff has kinetic energy falling at some v
* **Potential Energy (Ep)** is stored energy , due to an objects height or molecules chemical reactions it can undergo.
  * ie. A stationary ball at top of cliff has potential energy because it *can* fall
### 1.9 Accuracy, Prescision, and Significant Figures in Measurement
* There is always some uncertainty in the value of a measurement. To describe the degree of uncertainty we use words Precision and Accuracy.
* **Accuracy** refers to how close the true value a given measurement is
* **Precision** referes to how well a number of independent measurements agree with another.
* To indicate the uncertainty in a measurement, *the value you record should use all the digits you are sure of plus one additional digit that you estimate*
###### Significant Figures
* Defined as the total number of digits recorded for a measurement.
* To determine how many significant figures refer to the following.
  1. Zeros in the middle of a number are like any other digit, they are always significant.
  2. Zeros at the beginning of a number are not significant, they act only to locate the decimal point.
  3. Zeros at the end of a number and after the decimal point are always significant.
  4. Zeros at the end of a number and before the decimal point may or may not be signficant. The smaller the number, more likely to be significant, because the range if ignored becomes more drastic.
### 1.10 Significant Figures in Calculations
* Your final answer only has as many significant figures as your least precise measurement.
* How do you determine how many figures to keep and ignore?
  1. In carrying out multiplicaiton or division, the answer can't have more significant figures than either of the original numbers.
  2. Iin carrying out an addition or subtraction, the answer can't have more digits to the right of the decimal than either of the original numbers.
* Further more the rules for rounding off numbers are as follows:
  1. IF the first digit you remove is less than 5, round down by dropping it and all following digits.
  2. If the first digit you remove is 5 or greater, round up by adding 1 to the digit on the left.
