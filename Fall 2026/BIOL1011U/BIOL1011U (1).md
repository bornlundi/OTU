## 1. General course summary

This course introduces the properties of cells and the major cellular processes of living organisms. The central themes are:

- Cell structure and function
- Cell energetics
- Cellular respiration
- Photosynthesis
- DNA structure and function
- Control of gene expression
- Cell division
- Genetics
- Evolution of multicellularity
- Applications connecting biology with physics, mathematics, and computer science

The course is designed for students who are not specializing in biology or chemistry. Lectures establish the foundational concepts, quizzes provide retrieval practice, assignments apply concepts to broader contexts, and the midterm and final exam assess mastery.

## 2. Course expectations

- Attend all in-person lectures; lectures are not normally recorded.
- Make your own lecture notes.
- Complete assigned readings and textbook work.
- Complete quizzes and assignments by their deadlines.
- Plan for at least **six hours of private study per week**.
- Seek help early through office hours, PASS, Science Café, peer tutoring, TAs, or other approved supports.
- Track your grades throughout the term.

## 4. Week-by-week study and action plan

> The syllabus gives a course-wide topic sequence but does not provide a complete dated lecture-by-lecture topic table in the available schedule section. The plan below follows the syllabus themes and the quiz/assignment milestones. Confirm exact lecture topics in Canvas.

### Week 1 — Course orientation and cell foundations
**Study**
- Review the course structure, expectations, and assessment rules.
- Begin cell structure and function.
- Review basic biological organization and the relationship between structure and function.

**Do**
- Obtain the textbook: *Biology: How Life Works*, 4th edition.
- Set up a notes system.
- Complete any Canvas orientation or trial quiz requirements.
- Start a running glossary of biological terms.

**Expect**
- Foundational terminology and diagrams.
- Emphasis on understanding rather than memorizing isolated facts.

### Week 2 — Cell structure, membranes, and transport
**Study**
- Organelles and their functions.
- Cell membranes and membrane organization.
- Movement of substances across membranes.
- Diffusion, osmosis, and active transport.

**Do**
- Draw and label a eukaryotic cell.
- Compare passive and active transport.
- Make flashcards for organelles and membrane processes.

**Expect**
- Questions that connect a structure to its function.
- Application of transport concepts to biological situations.

### Week 3 — Cell energetics
**Study**
- Energy in biological systems.
- ATP and energy coupling.
- Enzymes and factors affecting enzyme activity.
- Basic thermodynamic ideas relevant to cells.

**Do**
- Explain how ATP supports cellular work.
- Create a concept map linking enzymes, activation energy, and reaction rates.
- Begin Assignment 1 if Canvas materials are available.

**Expect**
- Conceptual questions involving energy transfer and enzyme behavior.

### Week 4 — Cellular respiration
**Study**
- Overall purpose of cellular respiration.
- Glycolysis.
- Pyruvate processing and the citric acid cycle.
- Electron transport and chemiosmosis.
- Aerobic versus anaerobic pathways.

**Do**
- Build a respiration pathway summary.
- Identify inputs, outputs, and locations of major stages.
- Prepare for Lecture Quiz 1.

**Expect**
- Process-order questions and questions about ATP production.

### Week 5 — Photosynthesis
**Study**
- Chloroplast structure.
- Light-dependent reactions.
- Electron transport and ATP production.
- Carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle.
- Relationship between photosynthesis and respiration.

**Do**
- Compare photosynthesis and respiration in a table.
- Explain the role of light, water, carbon dioxide, ATP, and NADPH.
- Review for Lecture Quiz 2.

**Expect**
- Questions connecting organelle structure to energy conversion.

### Week 6 — DNA structure and replication
**Study**
- DNA structure and complementary base pairing.
- DNA replication.
- Enzymes involved in replication.
- Accuracy and proofreading.

**Do**
- Draw a replication fork.
- Explain leading and lagging strand synthesis.
- Complete or begin Assignment 2.

**Expect**
- Sequence-based and mechanism-based questions.

### Week 7 — Gene expression
**Study**
- Transcription.
- RNA processing where relevant.
- Translation.
- Genetic code and mutations.
- Regulation of gene expression.

**Do**
- Practice moving from DNA sequence to RNA and then to protein.
- Distinguish transcription from translation.
- Prepare for the midterm.

**Expect**
- Questions requiring several linked steps rather than one definition.

### Week 8 — Midterm review and genetics
**Study**
- Review all pre-midterm material.
- Begin Mendelian genetics, alleles, genotype, phenotype, and inheritance patterns.

**Do**
- Complete mixed practice questions.
- Review quiz mistakes and unclear concepts.
- Write a one-page pre-midterm summary.

**Expect**
- The midterm covers the material assigned before the test. Check Canvas for the exact scope.

### Week 9 — Cell division
**Study**
- Cell cycle stages.
- Mitosis and cytokinesis.
- Meiosis.
- Chromosome number and genetic variation.
- Cell-cycle regulation.

**Do**
- Compare mitosis and meiosis.
- Draw the stages in order.
- Practice chromosome-number questions.

**Expect**
- Visual identification and comparison questions.

### Week 10 — Genetics and inheritance
**Study**
- Mendelian inheritance.
- Punnett squares and probability.
- Independent assortment and linkage concepts as assigned.
- Relationship between genes, alleles, and traits.

**Do**
- Solve inheritance problems.
- Explain why meiosis creates variation.
- Prepare for Lecture Quiz 4 or the next scheduled quiz.

**Expect**
- Application of rules to unfamiliar crosses.

### Week 11 — Regulation of gene expression
**Study**
- Why different cells express different genes.
- Regulatory DNA and regulatory proteins.
- Gene-expression control in different biological contexts.

**Do**
- Make a flowchart from signal to gene-expression response.
- Review Assignment 3 requirements.

**Expect**
- Questions about how gene expression changes cell behavior.

### Week 12 — Multicellularity and biological organization
**Study**
- Evolution of multicellularity.
- Cell specialization.
- Communication and cooperation among cells.
- Trade-offs and advantages of multicellular organization.

**Do**
- Connect cell communication, specialization, and gene regulation.
- Complete Assignment 3 if due.
- Begin cumulative review.

**Expect**
- Integrative questions linking several course units.

### Week 13 — Integration and final preparation
**Study**
- Revisit the entire course, especially material after the midterm.
- Review respiration, photosynthesis, DNA, gene expression, division, genetics, and multicellularity.

**Do**
- Complete Assignment 4 by Nov. 23.
- Complete Quiz 5 and Quiz 6 by their deadlines.
- Build a final-exam checklist.
- Practice under timed conditions.

**Expect**
- Final-exam preparation and cumulative connections across topics.

### Final exam period — December 9–19

- The exam is cumulative, with emphasis on post-midterm material.
- Plan to write in person using Canvas and Respondus LockDown Browser.
