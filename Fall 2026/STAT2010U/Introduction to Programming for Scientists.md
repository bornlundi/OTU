## 1. General course summary

STAT2010U introduces statistics and probability for collecting, presenting, analyzing, and interpreting data and making decisions under variability.

The course covers:

- Data, populations, and samples
- Histograms, stem-and-leaf plots, and boxplots
- Measures of center and variability
- Quartiles, percentiles, and outliers
- Bivariate and multivariate data
- Correlation and causation
- Pearson correlation
- Least-squares regression and residuals
- Probability, sample spaces, and events
- Conditional probability and independence
- Random variables and probability distributions
- Binomial, normal, uniform, exponential, and related distributions
- Mean and variance
- Sampling distributions
- Central Limit Theorem
- Confidence intervals
- Hypothesis testing
- Type I and Type II errors
- p-values and statistical significance
- Tests for one mean and differences between means
- Paired, independent, and pooled data
- One-way ANOVA
- Experimental design
- Correlation and regression in R Studio

## 5. Statistical problem-solving checklist

1. Identify the type of data.
2. State what the population and sample are.
3. Visualize or summarize the data.
4. Check assumptions.
5. Identify the correct probability distribution or statistical procedure.
6. Define parameters and hypotheses.
7. Calculate the statistic or interval.
8. Interpret the result in context.
9. Distinguish statistical significance from practical importance.
10. If using R Studio, explain what the output means rather than copying it without interpretation.
